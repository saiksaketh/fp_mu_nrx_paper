\section{Quantization-Aware Neural Receiver}
\label{sec:nrx_qat}

We compress a 5G~NR-compliant multi-user uplink neural receiver using
the weight-only \gls{QAT} and magnitude-pruning operators of
Section~\ref{sec:qat_pruning}. The underlying detector is a
convolutional graph neural network (CGNN) designed for flexible
MU-\gls{MIMO} \gls{PUSCH} reception \cite{scamnrx}; related real-time
deployment studies motivate tight memory budgets
\cite{wiesmayr2024design}. Our focus is not on redesigning that
architecture, but on making it amenable to low-bit integer and
floating-point weights---with and without 50\% sparsity---while
preserving link-level \gls{BLER}.

\subsection{Uplink Setup}
\label{sec:system_model}
$U$ single-layer \glspl{UE} share the same time--frequency resources
toward a base station with $B$ antennas. After OFDM demodulation, each
resource element $(s,t)$ observes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mu_mimo}
  \mathbf{y}_{s,t}
  = \sum_{u=1}^{U}
    \mathbf{H}_{s,t,u}\,\mathbf{x}_{s,t,u}
    + \mathbf{n}_{s,t},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{n}_{s,t}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},N_0\mathbf{I}),
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{y}_{s,t}\in\mathbb{C}^{B}$. Payload bits are
\gls{LDPC}-encoded and mapped to \gls{QAM} symbols; known
\gls{DMRS} tones enable an initial least-squares channel estimate.
The neural receiver consumes the full post-\gls{FFT} slot (data and
pilots) and emits per-bit \glspl{LLR} for a conventional 5G~NR
decoder.

Unless stated otherwise we evaluate $U{=}2$, $B{=}4$, carrier
$2.14$\,GHz, $30$\,kHz subcarrier spacing, and MCS index~14
(16-\gls{QAM}, $R\approx0.48$) from Table~1 of \cite{3gpp38214}.
\gls{DMRS} uses configuration type~1, mapping type~A, and one
additional DMRS position. Training sees randomized 3GPP \gls{UMi}
channels with $1$--$2$ active users and UE speeds drawn uniformly from
$[0,56]$\,m/s. Evaluation uses the dual-user DoubleTDL-low scenario of
\cite{scamnrx} on a $132$-\gls{PRB} grid: the two users experience
distinct 3GPP TR~38.901 \gls{TDL} profiles under low antenna
correlation---UE~1 on TDL-B100/400 ($100$\,ns delay spread,
$400$\,Hz Doppler, $\approx\!56$\,m/s at $2.14$\,GHz) and UE~2 on
TDL-C300/100 ($300$\,ns, $100$\,Hz, $\approx\!14$\,m/s)---and the
receiver observes their superposition plus AWGN. Key parameters are
collected in Table~\ref{tab:sim_params}.

\subsection{Receiver Used in This Work}
\label{sec:architecture}
We build on the CGNN receiver of \cite{scamnrx}: a small input CNN
embeds each resource element into a state of dimension $d_s{=}56$;
$N_{\mathrm{it}}{=}8$ unrolled blocks then mix information across
users (dense aggregation) and across the time--frequency grid
(separable convolutions); a final dense readout produces \glspl{LLR}.
Separable layers factor into a depthwise spatial filter and a
pointwise mixer---a split we exploit when pruning
(Section~\ref{sec:pruning}). The same weights handle variable user
counts and bandwidths, which is why training on $4$ \glspl{PRB}
transfers to the $132$-\gls{PRB} evaluation grid. In the large
configuration used here, both the input and state CNNs have widths
$[128,128]$, aggregation uses width $64$, and the readout uses width
$128$.

For \gls{QAT}, every convolutional and dense kernel---depthwise,
pointwise, aggregation, and readout---is passed through the
fake-quantizer \eqref{eq:fake_quant} in INT8, INT4, FP8~(E4M3), or
FP4~(E2M1). Biases and activations stay in \texttt{FP32}. This
weight-only choice shrinks the dominant memory footprint while
leaving the arithmetic path unchanged, matching the deployment
setting targeted by low-precision tensor-core formats
\cite{micikevicius2022fp8}.

\subsection{Loss and Training Schedule}
\label{sec:loss}
Training maximizes bit-metric decoding quality under an auxiliary
channel estimate. If $B$ are the coded bits on data tones,
$\hat{L}$ the predicted \glspl{LLR}, and $\hat{\mathbf{h}}$ a
refined channel from a second readout, we minimize
\begin{align}
\label{eq:bce}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}}
  &= -\mathbb{E}\!\left[
       B\log\sigma(\hat{L})
       +(1-B)\log\!\bigl(1-\sigma(\hat{L})\bigr)
     \right], \\
\label{eq:mse}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}}
  &= \mathbb{E}\!\bigl[
       \|\hat{\mathbf{h}}-\mathbf{h}\|_{2}^{2}
     \bigr], \\
\label{eq:total_loss}
  \mathcal{L}
  &= \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}}
     + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}},
\end{align}
with $\sigma$ the sigmoid. Both terms are summed over all
$N_{\mathrm{it}}$ unrolled receiver iterations, and $\lambda$ decays
from $0.02$ to $0.01$ across two schedule phases. Each SGD step
draws a mini-batch of $N_B{=}128$ independently simulated OFDM
slots on-the-fly in Sionna \cite{sionna}, with random active-user
counts, velocities in $[0,56]$\,m/s, and rate-adjusted $E_b/N_0$
in a phase-dependent window of roughly $0$--$15$\,dB. The
\texttt{FP32} baseline is trained with Adam; quantized models always
warm-start from that checkpoint so that \gls{QAT} adapts an already
competent detector rather than learning detection and quantization
jointly from scratch.

\subsection{QAT and Pruned Fine-Tuning}
\label{sec:qat_protocol}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:qat_prune} summarizes the pipeline. Every
forward pass evaluates the neural receiver on $N_B$ OFDM slots under
fake-quantized weights \eqref{eq:fake_quant}; the \gls{STE} updates
the full-precision master copy. Optional magnitude pruning is applied
only after \gls{QAT} has converged, followed by a short masked
recovery fine-tune.

\textbf{Concrete schedules used in our experiments.}
All stages use $N_B{=}128$ slots per SGD step.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{FP8~(E4M3) / FP4~(E2M1) QAT:}
        $N_{\mathrm{QAT}}=5{\times}10^{4}+2{\times}10^{5}$
        iterations at learning rates $10^{-4}$ then
        $5{\times}10^{-5}$, i.e.\ $3.2{\times}10^{7}$ simulated
        slots in total.
  \item \emph{INT8 / INT4 QAT:}
        $N_{\mathrm{QAT}}=10^{6}+9{\times}10^{6}$ iterations at
        $10^{-4}$, i.e.\ $1.28{\times}10^{9}$ slots.
  \item \emph{Post-prune fine-tune} (all formats, sparsity
        $\rho{=}50\%$):
        $N_{\mathrm{P}}=5{\times}10^{3}+10^{4}$ iterations at
        $5{\times}10^{-5}$ then $2{\times}10^{-5}$, i.e.\
        $1.92{\times}10^{6}$ additional slots, with hard projection
        onto the prune mask after every update.
\end{itemize}
Depthwise kernels and the LLR readout are excluded from pruning;
pointwise and aggregation/dense kernels are pruned jointly with a
global magnitude threshold. The resulting ``QAT'' and
``QAT~$+$~pruning'' checkpoints are compared in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Weight-only QAT with optional magnitude pruning for a
neural receiver.}
\label{alg:qat_prune}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Pretrained \texttt{FP32} weights $\mathbf{W}$;
         target grid $\mathcal{G}$ (INT8/INT4/FP8/FP4);
         batch size $N_B$ (OFDM slots / step);
         QAT budget $N_{\mathrm{QAT}}$; optional sparsity
         $\rho\in[0,1)$ and prune budget $N_{\mathrm{P}}$
\ENSURE Quantized (and optionally sparse) weights
        $\mathbf{W}^{\star}$
\STATE Insert per-channel abs-max fake-quantizers
       \eqref{eq:fake_quant} on all CNN/MLP kernels
\FOR{$t = 1$ \TO $N_{\mathrm{QAT}}$}
  \STATE Sample $N_B$ OFDM slots
         $(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{h},\mathbf{b})\!\sim\!\mathcal{D}$
         (random users, UMi channels, $E_b/N_0$)
  \STATE $\widehat{\mathbf{W}} \leftarrow
         F_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{W})$
         \COMMENT{forward fake-quant}
  \STATE $\hat{L},\hat{\mathbf{h}} \leftarrow
         f(\mathbf{y};\,\widehat{\mathbf{W}})$
  \STATE Compute $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{b},\hat{L},\hat{\mathbf{h}})$
         via \eqref{eq:total_loss}; update $\mathbf{W}$ with
         Adam + \gls{STE}
\ENDFOR
\IF{$\rho > 0$}
  \STATE Build global magnitude mask $\mathbf{M}$ at
         sparsity $\rho$ on pointwise/dense kernels
         \eqref{eq:prune_mask}; set
         $\mathbf{W}\leftarrow\mathbf{M}\odot\mathbf{W}$
  \FOR{$t = 1$ \TO $N_{\mathrm{P}}$}
    \STATE Sample $N_B$ slots; fake-quant forward as above
    \STATE Adam + \gls{STE} update; project
           $\mathbf{W}\leftarrow\mathbf{M}\odot\mathbf{W}$
  \ENDFOR
\ENDIF
\STATE \textbf{return}
       $\mathbf{W}^{\star}\leftarrow F_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{W})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
)
