In this section, we evaluate the proposed neural receiver under a joint
compression stack: weight \gls{QAT} at FP8~(E4M3), INT8, FP4~(E2M1), and
INT4; INT8 asymmetric post-\gls{ReLU} activation quantization (W$b$A8);
and 50\% magnitude pruning on the QAT weights. Evaluation uses the
3GPP-compliant two-user PUSCH setup of Table~\ref{tab:sim_params},
following Algorithm~\ref{alg:qat_prune} with the optimizer budgets
listed there.
We report \gls{BLER} versus rate-adjusted $E_b/N_0$ at the $10\%$ and
$1\%$ operating points. All quantized and pruned receivers are compared
against the full-precision (\texttt{FP32}) neural receiver, LS channel
estimation with LMMSE equalization, LMMSE channel estimation with
K-Best detection, and an ideal K-Best receiver with perfect CSI
(Fig.~\ref{fig:BLER}). An analytic complexity model of the deployed
graph complements the radio results.

\subsection{Activation Quantization is Radio-Neutral at 8~Bits}
\label{sec:res_a8}
Figure~\ref{fig:bler_qat} shows \gls{BLER} for the unpruned QAT
receivers under joint weight+activation fake-quantization; the
corresponding weight-only operating points are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:wo_vs_a8}. Eight-bit weight quantization is effectively
lossless: FP8~(E4M3) and INT8, with or without INT8 asymmetric
post-\gls{ReLU} activations, match the \texttt{FP32} receiver to within
$0.05$\,dB at $10\%$ and $1\%$ \gls{BLER}. Relative to LS--LMMSE, both
8-bit receivers retain an SNR advantage of approximately
$1.85$--$1.95$\,dB and remain within $\sim$0.2\,dB of LMMSE--K-Best.
Activation quantization therefore adds the arithmetic and bandwidth
gains of 8-bit activations without a measurable radio penalty on this
MU-MIMO link. Because W$b$A8 matches weight-only \gls{BLER}, the
pruning results below---measured on the QAT weight
checkpoints---characterize the sparse operating point of the same
joint stack: weight precision, INT8 activations, and 50\% sparsity.

\subsection{FP4 QAT vs.\ INT4 QAT}
\label{sec:res_fp4}
At 4-bit precision the choice of format dominates
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bler_qat}, Table~\ref{tab:wo_vs_a8}). FP4~(E2M1) attains
$10\%$ and $1\%$ \gls{BLER} with an SNR gap of $1.32$\,dB and
$1.36$\,dB over \texttt{FP32}, while still outperforming LS--LMMSE at
both targets. INT4 incurs an additional $1.84$\,dB at $10\%$ \gls{BLER}
and $2.43$\,dB at $1\%$ \gls{BLER} relative to FP4 at identical nominal
$8\times$ weight compression, and falls behind LS--LMMSE. The
non-uniform E2M1 grid therefore approximately halves the 4-bit penalty of a
uniform integer grid. The same FP4--INT4 ordering is preserved under
W$b$A8.

After the same per-channel abs-max normalization used in \gls{QAT}
\eqref{eq:absmax_scale}, the $404{,}224$ trained \texttt{FP32}
pointwise and dense weights have excess kurtosis $3.97$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:weight_hist}). Of these, $65\%$ lie below the first
nonzero INT4 magnitude ($1/7\approx0.14$), while FP4 adds a level at
$1/12\approx0.08$. The interval $|w|\leq0.25$ contains $79\%$ of the
weights but only two INT4 magnitudes, including zero, versus four FP4
magnitudes ($0$, $0.08$, $0.17$, and $0.25$). The E2M1 grid therefore
resolves the body of the distribution more finely, allowing
\gls{QAT} to adapt weights to levels unavailable under uniform INT4.

\subsection{QAT with Magnitude Pruning}
\label{sec:res_prune}
Figure~\ref{fig:bler_prune} repeats the comparison after 50\% global
magnitude pruning of pointwise and dense kernels (depthwise and
readout layers left dense). For 8-bit formats the pruning overhead is
small ($\lesssim$0.1\,dB at both \gls{BLER} targets), and both pruned
receivers remain competitive with LMMSE--K-Best. Pruned FP4~(E2M1)
remains within $0.1$\,dB of unpruned FP4 and ahead of LS--LMMSE:
once the E2M1 grid has been adapted by \gls{QAT}, 50\% sparsity
incurs a negligible additional SNR cost. Pruned INT4 degrades by
approximately $1$\,dB relative to unpruned INT4 and trails LS--LMMSE
by more than $2$\,dB. Combining 4-bit integer quantization with
magnitude pruning is therefore unreliable, whereas FP4 \gls{QAT}
absorbs the same sparsity with negligible radio impact.

Figure~\ref{fig:sparsity_layers} reports the per-layer sparsity
realized by a global $50\%$ magnitude threshold. The threshold does
not produce $50\%$ zeros in every layer: INT4 systematically
over-prunes the residual writes of width $d_s{=}56$ that close each
CGNN block onto the skip connection. After \gls{QAT}, a large fraction
of the surviving INT4 coefficients have small magnitude, so the
$50\%$ sparsity budget is realized predominantly by zeroing
residual-path coefficients. On the same layers, FP4 sparsity is
comparable to INT8, indicating that the 4-bit microformat preserves a
magnitude profile that the global threshold does not thin
disproportionately. Combined with the coarse INT4 body grid of
Fig.~\ref{fig:weight_hist}, this
accounts for the \gls{BLER} degradation of pruned INT4, whereas
pruned FP4 remains within $0.1$\,dB of unpruned FP4.

Table~\ref{tab:complexity} summarizes the cost of the joint stack on
the instantiated evaluation graph (132~\glspl{PRB}, 2~users,
8 iterations). The receiver performs $19.22$\,G\,\glspl{MAC} per slot,
of which $76\%$ come from $1{\times}1$ pointwise convolutions---the
dominant GEMM-like operator targeted by low-bit quantization.
With INT8 asymmetric activations, 8-bit weights reduce bit-operations
by $16\times$ and the real weight footprint by $3.8\times$; 4-bit
weights reach $7.1\times$ compression, with a further reduction in
storage and \glspl{BOP} under 50\% magnitude pruning. Thus the joint
stack delivers substantial analytic reductions in weight storage,
activation volume, and bit-operations while retaining
near-\texttt{FP32} \gls{BLER} at 8~bits.
