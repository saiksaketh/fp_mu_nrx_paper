In this section, we evaluate the proposed neural receiver under a joint
compression stack: weight \gls{QAT} at FP8~(E4M3), INT8, FP4~(E2M1), and
INT4; INT8 asymmetric post-\gls{ReLU} activation quantization (W$b$A8);
and 50\% magnitude pruning on the QAT weights. Evaluation uses a
3GPP-compliant two-user PUSCH setup on DoubleTDL-low
(Table~\ref{tab:sim_params}): UE~1 follows TDL-B100/400
($400$\,Hz Doppler) and UE~2 follows TDL-C300/100 ($100$\,Hz).
We report block error rate (BLER)
versus rate-adjusted $E_b/N_0$, and center the discussion on the SNR
required to reach $10\%$ and $1\%$ BLER. All quantized and pruned
receivers are compared against the full-precision (\texttt{FP32}) neural
receiver, LS channel estimation with LMMSE equalization, LMMSE channel
estimation with K-Best detection, and an ideal K-Best receiver with
perfect CSI. An analytic complexity model of the deployed graph
complements the radio results.

{\bf Quantization-aware training (weights).}
Figure~\ref{fig:bler_qat} shows BLER for the unpruned QAT receivers
under joint weight+activation fake-quantization; the corresponding
weight-only operating points are reported in Table~\ref{tab:wo_vs_a8}.
Eight-bit weight quantization is effectively lossless: FP8~(E4M3) and
INT8 reach $10\%$ BLER at $2.29$\,dB and $2.27$\,dB, respectively,
matching the FP32 operating point of $2.30$\,dB to within $0.03$\,dB;
at $1\%$ BLER the same formats require $3.52$\,dB and $3.48$\,dB versus
$3.58$\,dB for FP32. Relative to the practical LS--LMMSE baseline
($4.14$\,dB / $5.43$\,dB at $10\%$ / $1\%$ BLER), both 8-bit QAT
receivers retain an SNR advantage of approximately $1.85$--$1.95$\,dB
and remain within $\sim$0.2\,dB of the stronger LMMSE--K-Best detector.

At 4-bit precision the choice of format dominates. FP4~(E2M1) attains
$10\%$ BLER at $3.62$\,dB and $1\%$ BLER at $4.93$\,dB---an SNR gap of
$1.32$\,dB and $1.36$\,dB over FP32---while still outperforming LS--LMMSE
by $0.52$\,dB and $0.50$\,dB at the two targets. In contrast, INT4
requires $5.46$\,dB and $7.36$\,dB ($+3.16$\,dB / $+3.78$\,dB versus
FP32) and falls $1.32$--$1.93$\,dB \emph{behind} LS--LMMSE. Relative to
FP4, INT4 incurs an additional $1.84$\,dB at $10\%$ BLER and $2.43$\,dB at
$1\%$ BLER at identical nominal $8\times$ weight compression. The
non-uniform E2M1 grid therefore roughly halves the 4-bit penalty of a
uniform integer grid and preserves the neural receiver's gain over
conventional LS-based detection.

{\bf Activation quantization is radio-neutral at 8~bits.}
Table~\ref{tab:wo_vs_a8} compares each weight format with its W$b$A8
counterpart (INT8 asymmetric post-\gls{ReLU} fake-quantization).
INT8+A8 and FP8+A8 reach $10\%$ BLER at $2.31$\,dB and $2.30$\,dB---within
$0.04$\,dB of their weight-only parents and of FP32---and the gap at
$1\%$ BLER remains $\le 0.05$\,dB. FP4+A8 likewise tracks weight-only
FP4 to within $0.04$\,dB at both targets. Activation quantization
therefore adds the arithmetic and bandwidth gains of 8-bit activations
without a measurable radio penalty on this MU-MIMO link. Because W$b$A8
matches weight-only BLER, the pruning results below---measured on the
QAT weight checkpoints---characterize the sparse operating point of the
same joint stack: weight precision, INT8 activations, and 50\%
sparsity.

{\bf QAT with magnitude pruning.}
Figure~\ref{fig:bler_prune} repeats the comparison after 50\% global
magnitude pruning of pointwise and dense kernels (194{,}176 of 388{,}352
pruned weights removed; depthwise and readout layers left dense). For
8-bit formats the pruning overhead is small: FP8 and INT8 reach $10\%$
BLER at $2.37$\,dB and $2.36$\,dB ($+0.07$\,dB / $+0.06$\,dB versus FP32;
$+0.08$\,dB / $+0.09$\,dB versus their unpruned QAT counterparts) and
$1\%$ BLER at $3.59$\,dB and $3.61$\,dB. Both pruned 8-bit receivers still
outperform LS--LMMSE by approximately $1.77$--$1.83$\,dB at $10\%$ BLER
and remain competitive with LMMSE--K-Best.

At 4 bits the formats again diverge under sparsity. Pruned FP4~(E2M1)
reaches $10\%$ and $1\%$ BLER at $3.55$\,dB and $4.83$\,dB---within
$0.1$\,dB of unpruned FP4 and still $0.59$--$0.60$\,dB ahead of
LS--LMMSE---indicating that 50\% sparsity is essentially free once the
E2M1 grid has been adapted by QAT. Pruned INT4, however, degrades
sharply to $6.41$\,dB and $8.65$\,dB at the two targets
($+0.95$\,dB / $+1.29$\,dB over unpruned INT4; $+4.11$\,dB / $+5.08$\,dB
over FP32) and trails LS--LMMSE by more than $2$\,dB. Combining aggressive
integer quantization with pruning is therefore unreliable, whereas FP4
QAT absorbs the same sparsity with negligible radio impact.

{\bf FP8/INT8 QAT vs.\ FP32.}
The near-identical $10\%$ and $1\%$ BLER operating points of FP8~(E4M3),
INT8, their W$b$A8 counterparts, and FP32---gaps below $0.1$\,dB with or
without 50\% pruning---show that 8-bit joint weight--activation QAT is
sufficient for this MU-MIMO receiver. At this precision the STE
fake-quantization, per-channel abs-max weight scales, and asymmetric
INT8 activation quantizers introduce no material distribution shift
relative to full precision, so real weight compression of about
$3.8\times$ (and an additional storage reduction from 50\% sparsity for
the pruned variants) comes at virtually no SNR cost, while INT8
activations unlock the integer arithmetic path.

{\bf FP4 QAT vs.\ INT4 QAT.}
A clear SNR gap separates FP4~(E2M1) from INT4 at both BLER targets:
$\sim$1.8\,dB without pruning and $\sim$2.9\,dB with 50\% pruning at
$10\%$ BLER. QAT adapts weights to the non-uniform E2M1 levels, which
allocate resolution near zero and match the heavy-tailed kernel
histograms of the convolutional graph neural receiver; uniform INT4
quantization cannot recover the same dynamic range at four bits. The gap
widens under pruning because magnitude sparsification removes small
weights that INT4 already represents poorly, whereas FP4 retains usable
resolution for the surviving coefficients. The same FP4--INT4 ordering
is preserved under W$b$A8 (Table~\ref{tab:wo_vs_a8}).

{\bf Pruning under QAT.}
Across formats, 50\% magnitude pruning is nearly transparent at 8 bits
($\lesssim$0.12\,dB at $1\%$ BLER) and even slightly beneficial for FP4
within Monte Carlo variation, but catastrophic for INT4. This asymmetry
underscores that sparsity and ultra-low-bit quantization must be
co-designed: once the weight grid is already information-starved, as with
INT4, further zeroing of coefficients compounds inter-layer error and
erodes the neural receiver's advantage over classical detection. FP4
QAT, by contrast, provides enough representational headroom for joint
weight compression and 50\% sparsity while remaining above the
LS--LMMSE baseline at both $10\%$ and $1\%$ BLER---and, given the
radio-neutral A8 results, this sparse FP4 operating point is compatible
with INT8 asymmetric activations in the same stack.

{\bf Analytic complexity.}
Table~\ref{tab:complexity} summarizes the cost of the joint stack on
the instantiated evaluation graph (132~\glspl{PRB}, 2~users,
8 iterations). The receiver performs $19.22$\,G\,\glspl{MAC} per slot,
of which $76\%$ come from $1{\times}1$ pointwise convolutions---the
dominant GEMM-like operator targeted by low-bit quantization.
Including FP32 biases and FP16 per-channel scales, 8-bit weights shrink
the footprint from $1708.5$\,KiB to $453.1$\,KiB ($3.8\times$), and
4-bit weights to $241.6$\,KiB ($7.1\times$). Activation traffic dominates
memory movement: $683$\,MiB of FP32 feature maps per slot versus a
$1.7$\,MiB weight store. With INT8 asymmetric activations, 8-bit
weights reduce bit-operations from $19.7$\,T to $1.2$\,T per slot
($16\times$); adding 50\% magnitude pruning further yields
$0.7$\,T\,\glspl{BOP}/slot at 8~bits and $0.3$\,T at 4~bits, with
sparse weight stores of $310.9$\,KiB and $194.1$\,KiB, respectively.
Thus the joint stack delivers substantial analytic reductions in
weight storage, activation volume, and bit-operations while retaining
near-\texttt{FP32} BLER at 8~bits.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Our study focuses on DoubleTDL-low with MCS~14 and reports analytic
graph complexity (storage, \glspl{MAC}, \glspl{BOP}) alongside link-level
\gls{BLER}. Extending the evaluation to richer MCS sets, higher antenna
correlation, and larger user loads, as well as broadening activation
coverage beyond post-\gls{ReLU} tensors, are natural next steps that
build on the operating points established here.
