\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{conclusion}
We studied joint weight and activation \gls{QAT}---and weight
\gls{QAT} combined with 50\% magnitude pruning---for a
standard-compliant MU-\gls{MIMO} 5G~NR neural receiver, comparing
integer grids (INT8/INT4) with floating-point microformats
(FP8~E4M3 / FP4~E2M1) and INT8 asymmetric post-\gls{ReLU}
activations. On the dual-user DoubleTDL-low uplink, 8-bit
weight+activation \gls{QAT} is effectively lossless: FP8+A8 and
INT8+A8 match both their weight-only parents and the \texttt{FP32}
receiver within $0.05$\,dB at $10\%$ and $1\%$ \gls{BLER}, while an
analytic cost model shows $3.8\times$ real weight compression and up
to $16\times$ fewer bit-operations per slot relative to
\texttt{FP32}. At 4~bits the format choice remains decisive, and the
reason is the kernel histogram: $65\%$ of per-channel-normalized
coefficients sit below the first INT4 level, so E2M1's extra
near-zero reconstruction points cover the body of the distribution
that a uniform 4-bit grid leaves unresolved
(Figs.~\ref{fig:weight_hist} and~\ref{fig:weight_hist_zoom}).
FP4~(E2M1) limits the gap to \texttt{FP32} to about $1.3$\,dB with
or without A8 and still beats \gls{LS}--\gls{LMMSE}; INT4 roughly
doubles that penalty. Under 50\% pruning the same asymmetry appears:
8-bit and FP4 receivers move by $\lesssim$0.1\,dB, whereas INT4
empties residual $d_s$ writes (up to $91\%$ sparse,
Fig.~\ref{fig:sparsity_layers}) and degrades by
more than $1$\,dB further. Taken together, these results
identify W8A8 / FP8+A8 as a radio-neutral operating point for
integer neural uplink inference, and FP4~\gls{QAT} as a practical
path to $7.1\times$ weight compression (with or without 50\%
sparsity) when additional rate is available.

Looking ahead, packing the trained E2M1 kernels into native MXFP4
inference paths is a natural export step: the codebook already matches
the MXFP4 element type \cite{ocp2023mx}, so the remaining work is
deployment packaging rather than a new radio design. Co-designing
structured (e.g., 2:4) sparsity with the quantization grid, and
validating the stack across broader channel and MCS conditions, are
likewise promising directions toward memory- and compute-efficient
neural uplink receivers for \gls{6G} \gls{RAN}.
