\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{conclusion}
We have presented joint weight-and-activation \gls{QAT}, optionally
combined with 50\% unstructured magnitude pruning, for a
standard-compliant MU-\gls{MIMO} 5G~NR neural receiver. At 8~bits,
FP8+A8 and INT8+A8 match the \texttt{FP32} receiver to within
$0.05$\,dB at $10\%$ and $1\%$ \gls{BLER}, with $3.8\times$ real
weight compression and up to $16\times$ fewer bit-operations per slot
(Table~\ref{tab:complexity}). At 4~bits, FP4~(E2M1) limits the gap to
\texttt{FP32} to approximately $1.3$\,dB and remains superior to
\gls{LS}--\gls{LMMSE}, whereas INT4 does not; the difference is
consistent with the concentration of normalized weights near zero
(Fig.~\ref{fig:weight_hist}). The same
ordering holds after 50\% pruning
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sparsity_layers}). The evaluation is restricted to
DoubleTDL-low at MCS~14 with post-\gls{ReLU} activation quantization;
richer MCS sets, higher antenna correlation, and larger user loads
are left to future work.

The trained E2M1 codebook already coincides with the MXFP4 element
type \cite{ocp2023mx}, so remaining work on native MXFP4 inference is
packaging rather than a new radio design. Combining the quantization
grid with structured sparsity (e.g., 2:4) is a further step toward
memory- and compute-efficient neural uplink receivers for \gls{6G}
\gls{RAN}.
