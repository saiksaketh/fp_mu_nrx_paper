\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{conclusion}
We studied weight-only \gls{QAT}---and \gls{QAT} combined with
50\% magnitude pruning---for a standard-compliant MU-\gls{MIMO}
5G~NR neural receiver, comparing integer grids (INT8/INT4) with
floating-point microformats (FP8~E4M3 / FP4~E2M1). On the dual-user
DoubleTDL-low uplink, 8-bit \gls{QAT} is effectively lossless: FP8 and
INT8 match the \texttt{FP32} receiver within $0.03$\,dB at $10\%$
\gls{BLER} and retain an $\sim$1.9\,dB gain over practical
\gls{LS}--\gls{LMMSE} detection. At 4~bits the format choice is
decisive. FP4~(E2M1) limits the gap to \texttt{FP32} to about
$1.3$\,dB at both $10\%$ and $1\%$ \gls{BLER} while still beating
\gls{LS}--\gls{LMMSE}; INT4 roughly doubles that penalty and falls
behind the classical baseline. The same asymmetry appears under
sparsity: 50\% pruning costs $\lesssim$0.1\,dB for 8-bit and FP4
receivers, but drives INT4 more than $1$\,dB further away. Taken
together, these results identify FP4~\gls{QAT} as a practical
operating point for joint $8\times$ weight compression and 50\%
sparsity on MU-\gls{MIMO} neural receivers, whereas ultra-low-bit
integer quantization alone is not a reliable path once pruning is
required.

Looking ahead, we plan to extend the pipeline to activation
quantization and native FP4/FP8 inference kernels (e.g., TensorRT on
tensor-core \glspl{GPU}), and to stress-test the pruned FP4 receiver
under higher-correlation channels, richer MCS sets, and larger user
loads. Co-designing sparsity patterns with the quantization grid---
rather than applying magnitude pruning post hoc---is a natural next
step toward deployable, memory-efficient neural uplink receivers for
\gls{6G} \gls{RAN}.
