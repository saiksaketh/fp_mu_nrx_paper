\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{conclusion}
Eight-bit weight--activation \gls{QAT} preserves link performance and
reduces modeled feature-map traffic in a standard-compliant 5G~NR
MU-\gls{MIMO} neural receiver. At 4~bits, 50\% pruning changes FP4 by
at most $0.1$\,dB. FP4 outperforms \gls{LS}--\gls{LMMSE}, whereas INT4
degrades sharply. Analytically, W4A8+P achieves $66\times$ and
$8.8\times$ reductions in bit-operations and weight storage,
respectively. This robustness aligns with FP4's denser near-zero grid
and lower residual-path sparsity.

Future work will co-design layer- and channel-adaptive precision,
residual-aware 2:4 sparsity, and native MXFP4 block scaling
\cite{ocp2023mx}, then measure hardware latency and energy across MCS
and user loads.
