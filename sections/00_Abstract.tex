\begin{abstract}
Deploying neural receivers requires reducing their memory traffic and
arithmetic cost without compromising link performance. We investigate
joint weight--activation \gls{QAT} and magnitude pruning for a
standard-compliant 5G~NR MU-\gls{MIMO} neural receiver. The evaluated
configurations use INT8, INT4, FP8~(E4M3), or FP4~(E2M1) weights,
INT8 asymmetric post-\gls{ReLU} activations, and optional $50\%$
unstructured sparsity. On a dual-user DoubleTDL-low uplink, the 8-bit
models remain within $0.05$\,dB of \texttt{FP32} at $10\%$ and $1\%$
\gls{BLER}; an analytic cost model yields up to $16\times$ fewer
bit-operations and $3.8\times$--$7.1\times$ real weight compression.
At 4~bits, FP4 incurs an approximately $1.3$\,dB gap to \texttt{FP32}
and outperforms LS--LMMSE at both \gls{BLER} targets, before and after
$50\%$ pruning, whereas INT4 degrades sharply.
\end{abstract}
