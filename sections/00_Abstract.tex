\begin{abstract}
Efficient neural-receiver deployment demands lower memory traffic and computation without sacrificing link performance.
We compress a standard-compliant 5G~NR \gls{MU}-\gls{MIMO} neural receiver using weight--activation \gls{QAT} and, separately, magnitude pruning.
We evaluate post-\gls{ReLU} INT8 activation quantization and $50\%$ pruning with INT8/INT4 and FP8~(E4M3)/FP4~(E2M1) weights.
On a two-user DoubleTDL-low uplink, 8-bit weight--activation models stay within $0.05$\,dB of \texttt{FP32}; activation quantization adds at most $0.04$\,dB at both \gls{BLER} targets.
Analytically, pruned 4-bit inference achieves $66\times$ bit-operation and $8.8\times$ weight-footprint reductions.
FP4 trails \texttt{FP32} by $\sim1.3$\,dB yet outperforms LS--LMMSE before and after pruning, with at most $0.1$\,dB pruning penalty.
This aligns with FP4's denser near-zero grid and lower residual-path sparsity.
\end{abstract}

Deploying neural receivers requires reducing their memory traffic and arithmetic cost without compromising link performance. We investigate joint weight-activation Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) and magnitude pruning for a standard-compliant 5G NR Multi-User Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MU-MIMO) neural receiver. The evaluated configurations use INT8, INT4, FP8 (E4M3), or FP4 (E2M1) weights, INT8 asymmetric post-ReLU activations, and optional 50% unstructured sparsity. On a dual-user DoubleTDL-low uplink, the 8-bit models remain within 0.05 dB of FP32 at 10% and 1% Block Error Rate (BLER); an analytic cost model yields up to 16× fewer bit-operations and 3.8×--7.1× real weight compression. At 4 bits, FP4 incurs an approximately 1.3 dB gap to FP32 and outperforms least-squares linear minimum mean-squared error (LS-LMMSE) at both BLER targets, before and after 50% pruning, whereas INT4 degrades sharply.
