\begin{abstract}
Neural receivers improve \gls{BLER} over conventional MU-\gls{MIMO} detection, but inference cost is dominated by weight storage and activation traffic. This paper presents \gls{QAT} for a standard-compliant 5G~NR neural receiver that combines integer and floating-point weight microformats (INT8/INT4, FP8/FP4) with INT8 asymmetric post-\gls{ReLU} activations and optional 50\% magnitude pruning. On a dual-user DoubleTDL-low uplink, 8-bit weight-and-activation \gls{QAT} matches the weight-only and \texttt{FP32} receivers to within $0.05$\,dB at $10\%$ and $1\%$ \gls{BLER}. An analytic cost model indicates up to $16\times$ fewer bit-operations and $3.8\times$--$7.1\times$ real weight compression. At 4~bits, FP4~(E2M1) remains substantially more robust than INT4 under both activation quantization and pruning.
\end{abstract}
