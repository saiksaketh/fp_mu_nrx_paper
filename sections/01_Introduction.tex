\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Neural receivers jointly learn channel estimation, equalization, and
demapping, and can improve \gls{BLER} over conventional receiver
pipelines \cite{deeprx_mimo_icc,scamnrx}. A real-time implementation
of the latter supports dynamic \gls{MCS} operation
\cite{wiesmayr2024design}.
Model-based neural receiver alternatives retain conventional signal-processing
structure to reduce computation and parameter count \cite{mahdi_mdx}.

Neural receivers can also be specialized to their deployment
environment. Fine-tuning on real-world 5G~NR measurements adapts the
receiver to local propagation and radio-frequency impairments while
reducing the sim-to-real gap \cite{nuri_reinhard_spawc}. Together with
real-time standard-compliant implementations, these results establish
deployment feasibility; memory traffic and arithmetic cost on
\gls{GPU}-class accelerators and \gls{NPU} ASICs remain key constraints
\cite{ran_accelerated_compute_reinhard}.

Weight quantization reduces parameter storage, whereas activation
quantization reduces convolutional feature-map traffic. Integer
\gls{QAT} preserves neural-receiver performance at moderate precision
\cite{saketh_qat}, but two deployment questions remain: whether
post-\gls{ReLU} activation quantization preserves link performance,
and whether 4-bit weights remain viable when combined with sparsity.
In particular, the relative behavior of integer and floating-point
microformats under these constraints has not been characterized for
standard-compliant \gls{MU}-\gls{MIMO} neural reception.

We address these questions using joint weight--activation \gls{QAT} for this
standard-compliant \gls{MU}-\gls{MIMO} receiver.
Per-channel INT8/INT4 and FP8/FP4 weight quantization is combined with
INT8 asymmetric post-\gls{ReLU} activations and optional global
unstructured magnitude pruning of $50\%$ of the eligible pointwise
and dense weights. The evaluation isolates the effects of activation
quantization and pruning at 8 and 4~bits against \texttt{FP32} and
conventional receiver baselines. On the evaluated multi-user
\gls{PUSCH} setup, 8-bit weights with INT8 post-\gls{ReLU} activations
are effectively lossless. At 4~bits, FP4 remains better than
\gls{LS}--\gls{LMMSE} and incurs negligible additional SNR penalty
after globally pruning half of the eligible weights; INT4 degrades
substantially under the same pruning rule. The 4-bit analysis relates the
FP4--INT4 \gls{BLER} gap to the normalized kernel distribution and to
the layer-wise sparsity induced by global magnitude pruning. An
analytic deployment model quantifies weight storage, activation
traffic, and bit-operation complexity.
