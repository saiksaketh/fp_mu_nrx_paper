\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Neural receivers jointly learn channel estimation, equalization, and
demapping, and can improve \gls{BLER} over conventional pipelines
\cite{scamnrx,deeprx_mimo_icc}. Real-time standard-compliant
implementations exist \cite{wiesmayr2024design}, model-based variants
reduce computation and parameter count \cite{mahdi_mdx}, and
fine-tuning on 5G~NR measurements closes the sim-to-real gap
\cite{nuri_reinhard_spawc}. Memory traffic and arithmetic cost on
\gls{GPU}-class accelerators and \gls{NPU} ASICs remain the main
deployment constraints \cite{ran_accelerated_compute_reinhard}.

Weight quantization reduces parameter storage and activation
quantization reduces feature-map traffic. Integer \gls{QAT} preserves
neural-receiver performance at moderate precision \cite{saketh_qat},
but two questions remain: whether post-\gls{ReLU} activation
quantization is radio-neutral, and whether 4-bit weights remain viable
under sparsity---including integer versus floating-point
microformats---for standard-compliant \gls{MU}-\gls{MIMO} reception.

We study joint weight--activation \gls{QAT} with optional $50\%$
unstructured magnitude pruning on this receiver, and relate the
4-bit \gls{BLER} gap to the normalized kernel distribution, the
layer-wise sparsity of a global magnitude threshold, and an analytic
model of storage, activation traffic, and bit-operations.
