\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Neural receivers (NRXs) have shown substantial \gls{BLER} gains over
classical channel estimation, equalization, and demapping in the
emerging \gls{6G} and \gls{AI}-\gls{RAN} setting
\cite{scamnrx,deeprx_mimo_icc}.
A standard-compliant \gls{MU}-\gls{MIMO} receiver built on the
architecture of \cite{scamnrx} further demonstrated real-time
inference and support for dynamic \gls{MCS}
\cite{wiesmayr2024design}.
In parallel, model-based hybrid neural receivers retain classical
signal-processing structure in order to reduce computational complexity
and the number of learnable parameters \cite{mahdi_mdx}.

A distinctive advantage of learned receivers is that they can be
adapted and fine-tuned to a deployment scenario, exploiting local
channel propagation and radio-frequency (RF) hardware impairments.
This has motivated recent site-specific neural receivers:
finetuning on real-world 5G~NR measurements closes the sim-to-real gap
\cite{nuri_reinhard_spawc}, while cell-site specialization has been shown to
adapt a neural receiver to local propagation and RF conditions
\cite{bell_labs}.
These results place NRXs on a trajectory toward practical \gls{RAN}
deployment, and shift the open question from \emph{whether} neural
receivers can work to \emph{how} they can be inferred efficiently on
\gls{GPU}-class accelerators or \gls{NPU}-based ASICs
\cite{ran_accelerated_compute_reinhard}.

Quantization is a primary lever for that inference cost.
Weight quantization reduces the memory footprint of the parameter
store; activation quantization relieves the memory-bandwidth pressure
of feature maps that dominate convolutional receivers.
Integer \gls{QAT} of neural receivers has already been shown to
preserve link-level performance at moderate bit widths
\cite{saketh_qat}.
This paper considers the state-of-the-art standard-compliant
\gls{MU}-\gls{MIMO} architecture of
\cite{scamnrx,wiesmayr2024design} under joint \gls{QAT} and
pruning across ultra-low-bit integer and floating-point microformats,
and quantifies why FP4~(E2M1) incurs a smaller \gls{BLER} penalty than
the integer-only inference of \cite{saketh_qat} at the same bit width.

\paragraph*{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A joint weight--activation \gls{QAT} pipeline for a
        \gls{MU}-\gls{MIMO} neural receiver, combining per-channel
        weight fake-quantization (INT8/INT4 and FP8/FP4) with INT8
        asymmetric post-\gls{ReLU} activations, optionally followed by
        $50\%$ magnitude pruning.
  \item A 5G~NR \gls{PUSCH} evaluation on DoubleTDL-low showing that
        W8A8 and FP8+A8 match the \texttt{FP32} receiver within
        $0.05$\,dB at $10\%$ and $1\%$ \gls{BLER}; that FP4~(E2M1)
        remains more robust than INT4  and classical LS-LMMSE under both activation quantization
        and pruning; and an analytic complexity model of weight
        compression, activation traffic, and bit-operations.
\end{itemize}
