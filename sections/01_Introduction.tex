\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Neural receivers jointly learn channel estimation, equalization, and
demapping and can improve \gls{BLER} over conventional pipelines
\cite{scamnrx,deeprx_mimo_icc}. A standard-compliant real-time
implementation has been demonstrated \cite{wiesmayr2024design}, model-driven variants
reduce computation and parameter counts \cite{mahdi_mdx}, and
fine-tuning on 5G~NR measurements mitigates sim-to-real mismatch
\cite{nuri_reinhard_spawc}. \glspl{GPU} provide programmable, adaptive,
real-time RAN compute; the reported neural receiver requires
$\sim26$\,TFLOP/s, motivating
quantization to reduce storage, traffic, and bit-operation complexity
\cite{ran_accelerated_compute_reinhard}.
Integer \gls{QAT} approaches
full-precision neural-receiver performance at 4~bits \cite{saketh_qat}.
We address two questions: whether post-\gls{ReLU} activation
quantization preserves link performance and whether sparse 4-bit
weights favor integer or floating-point microformats in
standard-compliant \gls{MU}-\gls{MIMO} reception.

We separately evaluate weight--activation \gls{QAT} and $50\%$
unstructured pruning. We show negligible SNR penalty from 8-bit
activations and FP4 robustness to pruning, explain the FP4--INT4 gap
through normalized weight distributions and residual-path sparsity,
and quantify storage, activation traffic, and bit-operations analytically.
