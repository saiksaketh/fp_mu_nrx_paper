\section{Quantization-Aware Training and Pruning}
\label{sec:qat_pruning}

Deploying a neural receiver under base-station memory and latency
constraints motivates joint reduction of weight precision and sparsity.
This section formalizes the two compression mechanisms used in our
experiments: weight-only \gls{QAT} with integer and floating-point
microformats, and unstructured magnitude pruning. Activations, biases,
and arithmetic in the forward graph remain in full precision
(\texttt{FP32}); only convolutional and dense kernels are quantized
and, optionally, sparsified.

\subsection{Weight-Only Fake Quantization}
\label{sec:fake_quant}
\gls{QAT} inserts a fake-quantization operator in the forward pass so
that the network experiences the same rounding and clipping noise that
low-precision inference will induce, while gradients update a
full-precision master copy of the weights
\cite{quantization_whitepaper,wu2020integer,jacob2018quantization}.
Let $\mathbf{W}$ denote a kernel (convolutional or dense) and let
$\mathcal{G}\subset\mathbb{R}$ be a finite quantization grid with
maximum magnitude $g_{\max}=\max_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|g|$. We use
\emph{per-channel abs-max} scaling along the output-channel axis:
for each output channel $c$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:absmax_scale}
  s_c = \frac{\max_{i}\,|W_{i,c}|}{g_{\max}},
\end{equation}
and the fake-quantized weight is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fake_quant}
  \widehat{W}_{i,c}
  = s_c\cdot\mathrm{round}_{\mathcal{G}}\!\bigl(W_{i,c}/s_c\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{round}_{\mathcal{G}}(\cdot)$ maps to the nearest
element of $\mathcal{G}$. Scales $s_c$ are recomputed from the live
weights on every forward pass (no frozen calibration set).

For integer \gls{QAT} with bit-width $b\in\{4,8\}$ we use a symmetric
narrow-range grid
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:int_grid}
  \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{INT},b}
  = \bigl\{-(2^{b-1}-1),\ldots,2^{b-1}-1\bigr\},
\end{equation}
so $g_{\max}=2^{b-1}-1$ and \eqref{eq:fake_quant} reduces to uniform
symmetric quantization with $2^{b}-1$ reconstruction levels
\cite{krishnamoorthi2018quantizing,wu2020integer}.

Because rounding is non-differentiable, the backward pass employs the
\gls{STE} \cite{bengio2013estimating,understanding_STE}, which
approximates
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ste}
  \frac{\partial\widehat{W}}{\partial W} \approx \mathbf{I}
\end{equation}
(for integer grids, gradients outside the abs-max clip range are
additionally zeroed). Weight updates are therefore applied to the
full-precision master $\mathbf{W}$, while every subsequent forward
pass re-quantizes through \eqref{eq:fake_quant}. The resulting
training objective is the task loss evaluated under simulated
quantization,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qat_objective}
  \min_{\mathbf{W}}\;
  \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{b})\sim\mathcal{D}}
  \Bigl[
    \mathcal{L}\bigl(\mathbf{b},\,
    f(\mathbf{y};\,\widehat{\mathbf{W}})\bigr)
  \Bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{y}$ is the received post-\gls{FFT} slot, $\mathbf{b}$
are the coded bits, $f(\cdot;\widehat{\mathbf{W}})$ is the neural
receiver with fake-quantized kernels, and $\mathcal{D}$ is the
training distribution (detailed in Section~\ref{sec:nrx_qat}).

\subsection{Floating-Point Microformats: FP8 and FP4}
\label{sec:fp_micro}
Low-bit floating-point formats allocate a non-uniform grid that
preserves dynamic range near zero---a useful match to the typically
heavy-tailed histograms of trained convolutional kernels
\cite{micikevicius2022fp8,rouhani2023microscaling}. We consider two
Open Compute Project (OCP) microformats natively supported by recent
inference hardware.

\textbf{FP8~(E4M3).}
An E4M3 value comprises one sign bit, four exponent bits, and three
mantissa bits. Following the OCP specification
\cite{micikevicius2022fp8,rouhani2023microscaling}, the finite grid
has maximum magnitude $g_{\max}=448$. Relative to INT8, E4M3 provides
a comparable bit budget with a non-uniform spacing that better covers
large dynamic ranges.

\textbf{FP4~(E2M1).}
An E2M1 value comprises one sign bit, two exponent bits, and one
mantissa bit, yielding the fifteen finite levels
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fp4_grid}
  \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{E2M1}}
  = \{0,\pm0.5,\pm1,\pm1.5,\pm2,\pm3,\pm4,\pm6\},
\end{equation}
with $g_{\max}=6$. At identical $8\times$ weight compression, E2M1
therefore offers a coarser but non-uniform alternative to INT4; both
formats use the same abs-max scaling \eqref{eq:absmax_scale} and STE
\eqref{eq:ste} during \gls{QAT}.

\subsection{Unstructured Magnitude Pruning}
\label{sec:pruning}
In addition to reducing bit-width, we sparsify kernels by unstructured
magnitude pruning \cite{song_han_1,kuzmin2023pruning}. After a
converged \gls{QAT} checkpoint is obtained, let
$\mathcal{T}$ collect the eligible weight tensors
(separable-convolution \emph{pointwise} kernels and aggregation/dense
kernels; depthwise kernels and the LLR readout are left dense). A
single global threshold $\tau$ is chosen so that a target fraction
$\rho$ of coefficients in $\mathcal{T}$ satisfy $|w|<\tau$. With a
binary keep-mask $\mathbf{M}$ of matching shape,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prune_mask}
  W^{\mathrm{pr}}_{i}
  = M_{i}\,W_{i},
  \qquad
  M_{i}=\mathds{1}_{\{|W_{i}|\ge\tau\}},
\end{equation}
we obtain an unstructured sparsity pattern with unchanged tensor
shapes. In our experiments $\rho=50\%$ (global magnitude pruning).
The pruned model is then fine-tuned under the same fake-quantization
operators \eqref{eq:fake_quant}, with hard projection onto
$\mathbf{M}$ after each optimizer step so that pruned coordinates
remain zero. This yields a joint low-precision and sparse receiver
without altering the computational graph topology.
